My research examines learning and educational technology through experimental, quantitative, and qualitative approaches.

## Independent Research Projects

### Evaluating the utility of GenAI as a retrieval practice tool: A comparison of student-, AI-, and experimenter-generated testing

Honors Capstone Research Project, University of California, Riverside | May 2025 - Present Faculty Advisor: Dr. Annie Ditta, Ph.D.

-   Evaluated the effects of testing type (student-generated, AI-generated, experimenter-generated) and question type (previously tested, previously not tested) on final test performance and students’ learning perceptions.

-   Designed, developed, and conducted a series of remote experiments using mock-classroom learning instruction.

-   Trained and managed a team of research assistants; taught them how to proctor pre-recorded lectures, learning assessments, and questionnaires via Zoom with screen share.

-   Developed a data cleaning and analysis workflow in R using machine learning to automate the coding process and minimize human error and researcher bias in coding.

-   Drafted the introduction and method for the manuscript; intend to complete and submit the manuscript to an educational psychology journal; registered to present the findings at the Annual Meeting of the Psychonomic Society.

### Investigating the relationship between cognitive load, comprehension, and flexible transfer in multimedia statistics learning: A classroom study

Independent Research Project, University of California, Riverside | May 2026 - Present Faculty Advisor: Dr. Annie Ditta, Ph.D.

-   Designed and developed a classroom study to translate the two-experiment research project, Sullivan & Ditta (ready to submit), to an ecologically valid instructional context.

-   Assessed 1) whether a quasi-manipulation of perceived extraneous cognitive load significantly predicts comprehension and transfer performance on a college statistics exam and 2) whether individual differences in perceived intrinsic, extraneous, germane, and total cognitive load significantly predict those same learning outcomes.

-   Wrote an IRB protocol and two preregistrations.

### Investigating the relationship between cognitive load, comprehension, and flexible transfer in multimedia statistics learning

Independent Research Project, University of California, Riverside | Sept 2024 - June 2026 Faculty Advisor: Dr. Annie Ditta, Ph.D.

-   Systematically evaluated the effect of low vs. high cognitive load on comprehension and flexible, content-grounded transfer of knowledge in an applied STEM learning context; assessed whether comprehension mediated any cognitive load effect on transfer.

-   Designed, developed, and conducted two laboratory experiments using mock-classroom instruction.

-   Trained and managed a team of research assistants to help with data collection.

-   Developed a data cleaning and analysis workflow in R for a mediation path model fitted with negative binomial regression to account for the over-dispersed, zero-inflated count outcome.

-   Used machine learning to efficiently code qualitative data and minimize human error and researcher bias in coding.

-   Published all study materials and R code on a ResearchBox repository; will be made public once the manuscript is accepted.

-   Drafted a manuscript for submission to a psychology research journal; learned how to effectively communicate my work to a lay audience through multiple oral and poster presentations.

-   Drafted a second manuscript for a secondary data analysis re-evaluating the factor structure of the cognitive load measure utilized in the experiments through a model comparison of two confirmatory factor analysis models.
